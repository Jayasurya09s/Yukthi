# Go-To-Market Strategy

## Overview

The AI Spend Audit tool is not designed as a traditional SaaS product with long onboarding flows or sales-heavy adoption.

The goal is:

* immediate value delivery
* viral sharing
* founder/operator curiosity
* lead generation for Credex

The product works best when users discover it organically through:

* founder communities
* cost optimization discussions
* AI tooling conversations
* “how are startups reducing burn?” threads

The strategy focuses on getting the first 100 high-intent users manually before attempting broader scale.

---

# Exact Target User

## Primary ICP

The ideal user is:

```txt id="llq0vk"
Head of Finance / FP&A Lead
at a Series A SaaS startup
with 10–80 employees
and $1M–$10M ARR
```

This person usually:

* owns budgeting and forecasting
* manages software/vendor spend
* prepares fundraising materials
* gets pressure from founders to reduce burn
* lacks visibility into AI tooling overlap

They are operational enough to try a new tool quickly without a long procurement process.

---

# Why This User Specifically

Most early-stage startups are rapidly adopting:

* ChatGPT
* Claude
* Cursor
* GitHub Copilot
* API-based AI tooling

But almost nobody internally owns:

* AI tooling governance
* optimization
* spend benchmarking

This creates a temporary gap where spend increases quietly without structured oversight.

The audit tool positions itself as:

```txt id="vwe96m"
“the easiest way to quickly identify wasted AI spend”
```

instead of:

```txt id="7s2rvl"
“another AI dashboard”
```

# Phase 5 — Referral Loop (Days 15–30)

The product should naturally encourage sharing.

Mechanisms:

* public audit URLs
* Open Graph previews
* “share your savings” screenshots
* referral incentives

Simple example:

```txt id="n0n7sx"
Invite another startup founder
and both receive a free optimization consultation.
```

The viral loop matters because:

* startup operators compare tooling constantly
* cost-saving screenshots are highly shareable
* benchmark curiosity spreads naturally

# Why This GTM Strategy Is Realistic

The strategy intentionally avoids:

* paid ads early
* generic SEO plans
* enterprise sales cycles
* over-engineered funnels

Instead, it focuses on:

* manual distribution
* founder-led growth
* highly targeted outreach
* fast feedback loops

This is the fastest way to validate whether:

```txt id="p6g3dn"
people genuinely care about optimizing AI tooling spend
```

before investing heavily into broader scale.

---

# Biggest GTM Risk

The biggest risk is not traffic.

It is user perception.

Many startups still treat AI tooling spend as:

* relatively small
* experimental
* non-critical

The product only works if users feel:

```txt id="v2tf2x"
the savings opportunities are meaningful enough to act on
```

That means the audit experience itself must feel:

* credible
* financially grounded
* immediately useful
* non-gimmicky

---

# Core GTM Insight

The most important realization during planning was:

```txt id="y8k0n4"
Founders rarely wake up wanting “AI infrastructure optimization.”

They wake up wanting to reduce burn,
extend runway,
and avoid wasting money.
```

The product positioning became much clearer after framing the tool around:

* operational efficiency
* burn reduction
* spend visibility

instead of generic “AI optimization.”
